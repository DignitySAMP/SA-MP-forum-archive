There are a lot of pre-conceived ideas as to what makes a good server, but it is exactly that which stifles success. Creating something successful is about creating something unseen, and unknown. Something unexpected is key to a bout of serious success. I'll give you an example, prior to vG:RP on MTA, MTA had never really seen a decent RP server. vG did something that people thought was impossible. Everyone wrote MTA off as a platform incapable of facilitating RP - but vG did it astoundingly well.

A SA:MP server that just point-blank forgets about what everyone has ever said and done before is one that will succeed. There's a lot of talk here about seeing the same thing over and over, and that's exactly right. When you read the server advertisements, it's peppered with systems, and features, but how about benefits? How about experiments? How about what that means to the player?

The focus of servers has very much moved away from the customer approach, and that is something that has to come around. Players are finite, and have requirements. Servers should be made for players, not players dragged into servers.

Something new, something fresh, something unheard of, un-thought of, unseen.
